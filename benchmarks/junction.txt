Reading
-------

The LP now enforces the regulatory minimum-release schedule at the
two terminal junctions of the NO5 cascade. BergheimKP at the end of
Hallingdalselvi requires 27.3–241.3 m³/s release; VangenSeaLevelKP
on the Aurland/Vangen system requires 0–70.5 m³/s. Both constraints
**bind throughout the horizon** — the LP's spill at every relevant
terminal reservoir (Limarka, Eikrebekken, Strandefjorden, Logga,
Ruståni feeding BergheimKP; Vassbygdvatn feeding VangenSeaLevelKP)
saturates at exactly the required minimum.

The soft slack `Rj_min` is **zero everywhere** — the cascade has
enough natural inflow to satisfy both regulatory minimums physically.
The soft-with-penalty pattern from the design (penalty 1e6 EUR /
m³/s · h) was correct insurance against infeasibility, but it
doesn't fire on this case.

The operational story is dramatic but physical:

- **Generation drops 56 %** because water that the LP would otherwise
  hold or turbine at the price peaks must now flow out through the
  regulatory minimum-release schedule during every hour, including
  the low-price ones.
- **In-cascade spill stays at zero.** The cascade has no actual dam
  overtopping; the LP routes every cubic metre to a turbine or to a
  terminal-junction outflow. The 18 417 m³/s·tsteps that pre-B1.7
  showed up as "Total spill" was always the regulatory **system
  outflow** at the two terminal junctions, not operational spill —
  the LP just had no way to label it correctly until typed
  junctions landed.
- **Objective drops by 5 MEUR (0.8 %)** — the cost of meeting the
  regulatory release. Without B1.7 the LP was free to drain the
  terminal reservoirs at non-regulated rates; with B1.7 the
  cascade must release the BergheimKP / VangenSeaLevelKP minimums
  every hour.

The B1.7 fix corrects an operational under-enforcement: real-world
Hallingdalselvi at BergheimKP can't be dewatered, and the LP must
respect that. The 5 MEUR objective drop is the *cost of physical
realism* — earlier numbers were artificially optimistic.

Sub-question: is this revenue loss recoverable?
-----------------------------------------------

Not within the LP's degrees of freedom — the LP is already choosing
the cheapest possible feasible spill schedule. To reduce the
revenue loss while honouring the regulatory minimum, an operator
would need to:

1. **Time-shift the minimum**. If the regulator allows daily or
   weekly averaging instead of hourly minimums, the LP could
   concentrate releases during cheap hours. The current YAML
   constrains hourly; a softer enforcement is a separate feature.
2. **Add downstream generation capacity**. If a new plant could
   turbine the regulatory release water on its way to BergheimKP,
   the spilled water becomes revenue. That's a capital-investment
   question, not an LP question.
3. **Use the cascade buffer more aggressively**. With multi-stage
   stochastic SDDP and proper water-value cuts, the LP might hold
   more water for high-price hours by accepting more spill at
   low-price hours. The current short-term LP doesn't have that
   visibility.

For the current B1.7 milestone the LP is correct; the 5 MEUR
"hit" is informational — it's the operational cost of the
constraint, not a model bug.
